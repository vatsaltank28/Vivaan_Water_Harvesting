What is rainwater harvesting (RWH)?

Rainwater harvesting means arresting rainwater during the monsoon and storing it in natural reservoirs (aquifers) or artificial ones (tanks). It can be used for flushing, washing, gardening, irrigation and, with treatment, consumption.

Rain is seasonal and short. In the long dry spell water is drawn from rivers, lakes and borewells, which lowers the groundwater level. Rain, the purest form of water, should be saved to recharge aquifers.

Is it an old practice in India?

Yes. The Chola kings collected rainwater from the Brihadeeswarar temple into the Sivaganga tank, and the Veeranam tank (1011 to 1037 CE) in Cuddalore stored water for drinking and irrigation. Temple tanks across South India still hold water when other sources dry up.

What are the types of RWH?

Rooftop RWH: terrace water is collected in a holding tank or pond, or recharged into the ground.

Surface RWH: runoff in storm water drains is diverted to recharging pits with borewells.

Why is it needed?
  • Meet ever-increasing water demand
  • Reduce groundwater pollution and improve its quality
  • Reduce soil erosion
  • Cut runoff that chokes storm drains and causes flooding
  • Supplement supply in summer and drought
  • Arrest the decline of groundwater levels
What are the advantages?
  • High-quality soft water, low in minerals
  • Bridges the gap between supply and demand
  • Lower pumping cost as the water table rises
  • Independent and reliable for isolated communities
  • A self-paying investment, recovered in a few years
  • In coastal areas it reduces salinity and protects the fresh-saline interface
Is RWH mandatory?

Yes, for buildings over 300 m² in Maharashtra. The Urban Development Department made it mandatory under Section 154 of the MR & TP Act, 1966 (Provision No. TPB 432001/2133/CR 230-01/UD-11, dated 10/3/2005). Municipal corporations require it for upcoming projects, and gram panchayats implement it with government support.

How much area does it need?

Approximately 15 to 20 m².

Can I harvest rain in an existing or old building?

Yes. A dry borewell, a row of soak pits or a concealed tank is enough, and rooftops and open ground act as the catchment.

Who will it benefit?

Individuals, societies, bungalows, hospitals, hotels and resorts, schools, colleges and industries: anywhere water need is high and availability is low.

How much will it cost?

It depends on your roof area and the structures used. RWH is inexpensive, easy to operate and self-paying, typically recovered in a few years against municipal or tanker water costs. At Easter Heights, Santacruz, a Rs. 3,50,000 system paid back in 4 to 5 years.

Who builds it and how long does it take?

You need an RWH consultant to design it; a skilled mason or plumber can build it. For new projects the builder hires implementing contractors. Duration depends on project size.

What does maintenance cost?

Minimal. Modern screens such as the Apollo Vee-Wire Filter Screen bring maintenance cost close to zero. Clean once a year before the monsoon.

Does it work?

Yes. Our ancestors harvested rain as naturally as they tilled the ground. Modern systems are more reliable. See our success stories.
